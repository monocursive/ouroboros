## Observation performance

Profiling the owned test subprocess identified ptrace stops and consumer wakeups
alongside allocation/serialization costs. Host perf restrictions were unchanged.
An allocation/clone candidate failed the predeclared improvement criterion and
was reverted: its 60-pair fileops ratio was 1.0039, with 95% interval
0.9889–1.0073. Its [patch](results/followup-2026-10-08/vps/rejected-allocation-candidate.patch),
[read/write trial results](results/followup-2026-10-08/vps/rejected-read-write/analysis.json)
and [fileops results](results/followup-2026-10-08/vps/fileops-paired/analysis.json)
are retained.

The retained change coalesces consumer wakeups for up to 250 microseconds after
the first syscall event, within the caller's remaining wait budget. Nonblocking
drains and first lifecycle events remain immediate. Every syscall stop is still
observed; queue bounds, byte charging and coverage rules are unchanged.

The final paired experiment uses 60 measured rounds plus five warmups,
randomized before/after order, the same fixture for both arms and 5,000
create/rename/unlink rounds per launch. There are 120 measured launches and ten
warmups, **zero exclusions**, maximum sampled one-minute load 1.586, and 10,000
paired-round bootstrap resamples.

| Metric | Before median | After median | Paired after/before, 95% interval |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| File-operation work | 993.28 ms | 921.29 ms | 0.9261; 0.9137–0.9452 |
| Whole command | 1,109.55 ms | 1,042.40 ms | 0.9398; 0.9281–0.9532 |

Work time improves **7.4% (5.5–8.6%)** for this workload and host. All 130 trace
hashes, receipt associations and exactly 1,950,000 expected file operations were
verified, with empty process trees and complete cleanup. This does not establish
the same speedup for model-driven agents.
